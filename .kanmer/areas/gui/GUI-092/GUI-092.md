## What

`scripts/release.mjs` packs the installer **twice** — step 7
(`electron-builder --publish never`) and step 9 (`--publish always`). NSIS output
is **not byte-reproducible**: the two packs produce installers of identical size
and different bytes.

The `latest.yml` left on disk described the **first** pack's installer while the
**second** pack's installer was the one uploaded. Measured on the real v0.3.3
release, 2026-08-17:

```
local exe sha512  KPPgLdA9C54HMKuQdq2oAtO7EdA1Q3VrVDtTYIdIA9BW…
latest.yml says   7td6VKB05kixJdaG6XNkL3sSmm9Zprmg1IJJlQl31RIW…
both sizes        78080687   (identical)
```

The published exe's sha256 matched the local exe exactly, and so did the
blockmap. Only the manifest was wrong.

## Why this is the dangerous one

A missing asset fails loudly. **This fails quietly, and late.** `electron-updater`
reads `latest.yml`, downloads the installer, hashes it, and compares. A mismatch
means every client downloads 78 MB and then **rejects it as corrupt** — the update
never installs and the error blames the download, not the manifest.

It is also invisible to every check that existed before this release: sizes match,
both files are present, both are `uploaded`, the tag resolves. Only comparing the
manifest's hash against the actual binary reveals it, which is exactly what
[[GUI-066]]'s `verify-release-assets.mjs` does — and it caught this on its **first
live run**, which is the reason v0.3.3 is not currently broken.

## Approach

- **Pack once.** The two packs exist because step 7 validates the package
  (`check-updater-package.mjs`) before anything is public and step 9 publishes.
  Investigate whether step 9 can publish the *already packed* artifacts
  (`--publish always` with a prepacked dir, or `electron-builder --publish` on the
  existing output) rather than rebuilding. That removes the divergence at its
  source rather than detecting it afterwards.
- If two packs are genuinely unavoidable, then **regenerate `latest.yml` from the
  installer that is actually published**, and assert the pairing before upload.
- Either way, keep the hash comparison in the rail. It is the only thing that
  caught this.

## Verification

- [ ] After a release, `latest.yml`'s `sha512` equals the sha512 of the published
      installer — asserted, not eyeballed.
- [ ] A deliberately mismatched manifest fails the rail. (`verify-release-assets`
      already does this; confirm it stays reachable — see the sibling ticket about
      publish aborting before verification runs.)
- [ ] An end-to-end update from the previous version actually installs, proving
      the manifest and the binary agree in the only way that matters.
